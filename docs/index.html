The Numbers
FY26 actuals: Copper 1,953 kt (guidance 1,900-2,000 kt); Iron ore 264.7 Mt (guidance 258-269 Mt); BMA coal 18.6 Mt (guidance 18-20 Mt); NSWEC 16.4 Mt (guidance 14-16 Mt). FY27 guidance: Copper 1,650-1,800 kt (sharp step-down of ~8-15% from FY26); Escondida 1,000-1,100 kt vs FY26 1,261 kt (-13% to -21%); Copper SA 290-320 kt vs FY26 321 kt; Iron ore 260-272 Mt; BMA 18.5-20.5 Mt. Net debt ~US$9bn. Jansen impairment ~US$2.3bn. Capex ~US$5bn. Antamina streaming proceeds ~US$4.3bn. Tax paid US$5.4-5.5bn. RCII funding US$100-150m charge. Effective tax rate lower half of 36-40% range. Escondida FY27 grade ~0.70% vs FY26 0.90% — significant grade cliff. Medium-term Escondida guidance 900-1,000 ktpa (FY28-FY31 average), implying FY27 1,000-1,100 kt is itself a transition year before further decline.
Why Happening
1. Escondida grade decline is the dominant copper volume driver — feed grade fell from 1.02% in FY25 to 0.90% in FY26, dropping further to ~0.70% in FY27, a 31% grade deterioration in two years. This is structural/geological, not operational. 2. Spence (Pampa Norte) down 21% on complex hypogene ore processing issues; concentrator upgrade not online until FY28. 3. Carrapateena underground conveyor belt failure post period-end (July 2026) adds up to 8 weeks production impact on top of planned smelter maintenance inventory build — compounding Copper SA FY27 guidance cut. 4. Jansen impairment of US$2.3bn signals reduced expected returns from the potash project despite 84% completion — no detailed explanation given. 5. WA Nickel remains suspended, still burning ~US$150m EBITDA negative in H2. 6. BMA geotechnical issues at Broadmeadow and cyclone weather disruptions partially offset by operational recovery.
Balance Sheet
Net debt guided to ~US$9bn at 30 June 2026. The US$4.3bn Antamina silver streaming proceeds are a financing inflow that materially cushions the balance sheet in H2 FY26 — without this, net debt would be ~US$13bn+. Capex of ~US$5bn plus Jansen Stage 2 (US$6.9bn total, 16% complete) and Ministers North approval (~US$0.9bn, 100% basis) mean capital commitments remain substantial. Tax payment of US$5.4-5.5bn is a large cash drain. The Jansen impairment (US$2.3bn) is flagged as pre- and post-tax, suggesting limited tax shield. No change to dividend framework disclosed but H1 FY26 dividend was ~US$3.7bn outflow. No incremental funding need flagged explicitly, but free cash flow generation will be tested in FY27 with lower copper volumes and ongoing Jansen Stage 2 spend.
Red Flags
  • Copper FY27 guidance of 1,650-1,800 kt is a 10-15% step-down from FY26 1,953 kt — largest single-year production drop in the portfolio's recent history, driven by Escondida grade falling to ~0.70%. This is not a one-year issue; medium-term guidance averages 900-1,000 ktpa at Escondida alone (FY28-FY31), implying the asset loses ~200 kt of annual production permanently at current mine plan.
  • Jansen US$2.3bn impairment on a project 84% complete and US$8.4bn total committed is deeply concerning — management provides no explanation of the impairment trigger, discount rate changes, or revised return assumptions. A feasibility-stage impairment of this scale on a near-complete project suggests potash market assumptions have deteriorated materially or project costs have blown out further.
  • Carrapateena conveyor belt failure (July 2026, post period-end) is an unplanned operational event adding to already reduced FY27 Copper SA guidance of 290-320 kt vs FY26 321 kt. The combination of this, smelter maintenance inventory build, and Prominent Hill weather-related sales deferrals suggests H1 FY27 copper volumes will be heavily back-end weighted.
  • AUD/USD realised at 0.68 vs guidance assumption of 0.65 — a 4.5% stronger AUD inflates reported unit costs in USD terms relative to guidance. Management neutralises this by reporting costs 'at guidance FX rates', obscuring the true cash cost headwind. At spot AUD, WAIO and BMA unit costs would look worse.
  • WA Nickel remains in suspension burning ~US$150m EBITDA negative per half with no restart timeline or divestment update disclosed — a stranded asset with ongoing cash drag and no resolution in sight.
  • Antamina copper FY27 guidance 120-140 kt vs FY26 152 kt (down ~18%) due to planned grade decline — a third major copper asset with volume headwinds simultaneously.
  • Samarco financial impact from dam failure still 'not yet finalised' after years — ongoing legal and remediation tail risk unquantified in this release.
Bottom Line
Operationally BHP delivered a solid FY26 — costs controlled, iron ore at records, copper at the top of guidance — but the forward picture is materially worse: FY27 copper production falls ~10-15% driven by a severe and structural Escondida grade cliff, three of four copper assets face simultaneous volume headwinds, and a US$2.3bn impairment on a nearly-complete Jansen with no explanation is a red flag management is actively downplaying. The headline 'record production' framing is misleading given the volume step-down already baked into FY27 guidance.
Key Questions
  • What specifically triggered the US$2.3bn Jansen impairment at 84% completion — is this a potash price assumption reset, cost overrun, or revised operational ramp-up timeline, and what is the revised IRR/NPV for Stage 1 and Stage 2?
  • Escondida grade drops from 0.90% in FY26 to ~0.70% in FY27 and medium-term guidance averages 900-1,000 ktpa — what is the expected grade recovery profile beyond FY31 and when does Los Colorados concentrator optimisation and leaching technology sufficiently offset the structural grade decline?
  • With copper production down ~300 kt in FY27 at roughly US$5-6/lb spot prices, what is the expected free cash flow generation for FY27 after capex (~US$5bn run-rate), Jansen Stage 2 drawdown, and dividends — and does net debt of ~US$9bn remain stable or does BHP need to cut returns to shareholders?
