Deal Summary
Aussie Broadband (ASX:ABB) acquired AGL Energy's telecommunications business (AGL Telco) for $115 million, paid entirely in ABB shares (22 million shares at 90-day VWAP to 9 Feb 2026), representing ~7% of ABB's post-issue capital. The deal brings ~350,000 NBN and mobile connections with migration to complete Q2 FY27, alongside a strategic distribution partnership with AGL's ~4.5 million customer base.
What They Bought
Primarily market share / subscriber scale — this is a customer book acquisition, not a technology or capability deal. AGL Telco had no proprietary network infrastructure; ABB is simply absorbing resold NBN and mobile connections onto its own wholesale platform. The 'strategic partnership' with AGL's 4.5 million energy customers is the speculative optionality layer, but the commercial terms of that partnership (exclusivity, duration, referral economics) are entirely undisclosed.
Price Check
Purchase price is $115 million in scrip for 350,000 connections, implying ~$329 per connection. At guided $21 million EBITDA post-migration, the EV/EBITDA entry multiple is ~5.5x, which looks optically cheap for a mature Australian telco. However: (1) that $21M is a management projection contingent on successful migration and full-year contribution — the actual as-acquired EBITDA of AGL Telco is never disclosed; (2) the scrip consideration at a 90-day VWAP may have been set at an inflated reference price if ABB traded at a premium pre-announcement; (3) no revenue figure for AGL Telco is provided, making independent per-connection economics impossible to verify; (4) the EBITDA is pre any amortisation of customer acquisition costs or partnership fees paid to AGL — the true cash yield is unknown.
Strategic Fit
Fit is real but narrow: ABB gets wholesale cost leverage from higher NBN connection volumes, which compresses per-unit CVC and access costs — this is the only concrete, quantifiable synergy. The AGL energy-bundle cross-sell is the headline pitch but is pure hand-waving at this stage: no conversion rate assumption, no exclusivity confirmed, no economics of the referral arrangement disclosed. The 500,000 connection 'ambition' from 350,000 current implies 43% growth with zero supporting data. Bundled energy-telco models have a mixed global track record and ABB has no prior energy retail experience.
Integration Risk
Migration is the primary near-term risk: ABB is simultaneously migrating More/Tangerine (~275,000 connections by June 2026) AND AGL Telco (~350,000 connections through Q1-Q2 FY27) — two large-scale migrations running concurrently or in close succession on the same platform. More migration is already 5% below prior estimate (275k vs implied ~290k). Customer churn during migration is structurally elevated in NBN switching events; no churn assumption or retention guarantee is disclosed. AGL Telco staff/systems disposition is not described. Dependency on AGL as a distribution partner creates a single-counterparty commercial risk — if AGL decides to re-enter telco or switches partners, the 4.5M customer funnel closes.
Balance Sheet
Structure is all-scrip so no cash outflow and no incremental debt; net debt/EBITDA remains at a stated 0.72x at May 2026, which is conservative. Dilution is ~7% to existing shareholders. Capex is guided to the upper end of $55-60M range for FY26, suggesting integration spending is already pressuring capex. The More/Tangerine wholesale agreement carries $5M in amortised incentives that reduce EBITDA contribution from $12M gross to $7M net — a similar incentive structure for AGL Telco cannot be ruled out and is undisclosed. No covenant or facility headroom detail provided.
Red Flags
  • AGL Telco's actual historical revenue and EBITDA are never disclosed — the $21M EBITDA is a post-migration management projection with no base to validate against
  • Commercial terms of the AGL strategic partnership (duration, exclusivity, referral fees, termination rights) are entirely absent from the announcement
  • Concurrent migration of two large customer books (~625,000 combined connections) on the same platform within 12 months — execution risk is additive, not independent
  • More/Tangerine migration already 5% below prior estimate at 275k vs guide, indicating early signs of churn leakage that may repeat with AGL Telco
  • No disclosure of AGL Telco customer ARPU, churn rate, or product mix (NBN vs mobile split) — impossible to independently stress-test the $21M EBITDA claim
  • AGL receives $115M in ABB scrip and becomes a ~7% shareholder, creating a misaligned incentive — AGL could sell down shares and simultaneously reduce partnership commitment with no disclosed lock-up period mentioned
  • 500,000 connection 'ambition' from AGL's 4.5M base implies ~11% penetration with zero disclosed timeline, pricing model, or contractual underpinning
  • Capex at upper end of guidance range while claiming 'capital-light model' is internally contradictory and warrants explanation
Key Questions
Bull: At ~5.5x guided EBITDA and paid in scrip with no leverage impact, ABB buys meaningful NBN scale that compresses wholesale unit costs, and the AGL distribution partnership — even at modest conversion — could add hundreds of thousands of connections at near-zero customer acquisition cost, driving a step-change in earnings by FY28.
Bear: The $21M EBITDA figure is unaudited management spin with no historical anchor; concurrent migrations of 625,000 connections risk material churn that destroys the acquisition economics; AGL has no lock-up and every incentive to monetise its scrip once migration is complete; and the energy-telco bundle thesis has failed repeatedly elsewhere, leaving ABB with an expensive, dilutive subscriber book and a 7% overhang from a disinterested strategic partner.
Key Questions:
  • What were AGL Telco's actual FY25 revenue, EBITDA, and churn rate, and what is the assumed post-migration churn rate underpinning the $21M EBITDA projection?
  • What are the specific commercial terms of the AGL strategic partnership — exclusivity, minimum referral commitments, duration, termination triggers, and any revenue-share or referral fee paid to AGL?
  • Is there a lock-up or escrow on the $115M in ABB shares issued to AGL, and what are AGL's obligations if it disposes of its shareholding or reduces its distribution commitment?
