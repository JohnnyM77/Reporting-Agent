## What Austco is

Austco Healthcare makes the nurse-call and clinical-communication systems that
hospitals run on — the buttons, terminals and software that connect a patient in
a bed to the caregiver who answers. Founded in Perth in 1986, it is now genuinely
global: over 4,500 installations across 56 countries. On top of the hardware sits
a recurring software and service-maintenance layer, and around it a roll-up
strategy of small, cheap bolt-on acquisitions.

It is the kind of dull, essential, embedded business I like — until the question
becomes whether it actually compounds, which is where this one currently sits.

## Why I bought (February 2025, at 30c)

The FY24 roadshow told a clean operating-leverage story: revenue up 39% to
$58.2m, EBITDA up 126% to $8.1m, gross margin above 52%, a recurring software
line, two acquisitions bedding in, and — the number that did the work — an
unfilled contracted order book of **$50.3m, up 72%** in a year, with new wins
outpacing delivery. A growing book on a profitable, cash-generative micro-cap,
apparently trading at deep value: buy the order book and let it convert.

## The uncomfortable part, and why it's the most useful line in this file

Part of why 30c felt so safe was a **Simply Wall Street DCF valuing AHC at about
$4.60 a share.** Against that, 30 cents looked like a fifteen-bagger waiting to
happen.

That number was garbage, and my own FY26 model now shows exactly why: SWS used a
**6.5% discount rate** — barely above the risk-free rate, for a project-lumpy,
80%-foreign-currency micro-cap — and **extrapolated a 60% jump in free cash
flow** that never came. On grounded assumptions the business is worth around 28
cents in my base case, not $4.60.

I don't think the SWS number was why I bought — the order-book case stood on its
own — but I let it make 30c feel like a one-way bet, and it wasn't. The lesson
is the point of keeping this record: **a broken valuation can dress a fair
purchase up as a steal, and the margin of safety you feel is not always the one
you have.**

## The record, and where it stands

Bought once, in February 2025, at **30 cents**. The stock sold off to around 22
cents after a poorly-handled June 2026 trading update, so the position is
modestly underwater — but on grounded numbers it is cheap, pricing in almost no
future growth. The ledger holds the figures.

## The pillar under strain

**P3 — the order book converting into organic growth — is STRAINED, and it is
the pillar the whole thesis rests on.** FY26 grew revenue 16% but almost all of
it was acquisitions; underlying NPAT was flat, 2H organic growth was just 3.4%,
and the order book only rose in constant-currency terms. None of that breaks the
business — the moat (P1), the roll-up discipline (P2) and the balance sheet (P4)
are all intact — but it leaves the compounding claim unproven.

So the resolution date is the 1H27 result, and the single number to watch is
whether overheads as a share of revenue start falling while organic growth picks
up. That is the difference between a compounder and a roll-up treading water, and
FY27 is where AHC has to show which one it is.
