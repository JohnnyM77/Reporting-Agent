## The finding, which is not the one I would have named

The instinct is that AR9 was a bad idea. The ledger says something narrower and
much more useful.

**The first buy is fine.** 5 cents in June 2020, 5 cents today, six years later.
A toe-hold in a speculative pre-profit company that went nowhere: an IRR of
-1.4% and about $1,000 of damage. That is what a correctly sized speculative
position looks like when it does not work.

**The second buy is the entire loss.** 94% of the capital went in at 30 cents —
six times the price of the first — in January 2021, after the story had run. It
is now worth 17 cents in the dollar and has cost roughly $193,000 on its own.

So the mistake was not the pick. It was buying a small amount when it was cheap
and unproven, then buying twenty times more after the price had multiplied by
six and *nothing about the commercial evidence had changed*. No new customer
cohort, no proof of repeatability — just a higher price and more excitement.

## The conflict, stated plainly

My brother was the CTO. He previewed the technology and he was right about it:
it works, it does what it claims, the controls really do travel with the
document.

That is precisely the trap. A demonstration from someone you trust, who built
the thing, is the most persuasive input available and it speaks to the wrong
question. I acquired genuine private information about *product quality* and
mistook it for information about *the investment*. The technology risk was the
one risk I had covered. The commercial risk — can anyone be persuaded to buy
it — was the one that mattered, and proximity told me nothing about it while
making me feel informed.

It also explains the sizing, which analysis does not. There is no version of a
written process that says "put a fifth of the portfolio into a 30-cent
pre-profit microcap because the price went up." Conviction that comes from a
relationship does not show up as a line in a spreadsheet; it shows up as a
position size.

`origin: HYBRID` is the technically correct field value. The honest reading is
that the idea was borrowed from a person I could not be objective about.

## Why this is graded C

The two 2025 Strawman posts are excellent, and if this file were about
2025 it would be a Grade A. But the decision that determined the outcome was
made in **January 2021**, and there is no contemporaneous record of it at all —
no written thesis, no valuation, no position-sizing rationale, nothing. Six
years of holding have produced good writing about a decision that was never
documented when it was made.

Grading this B on the strength of documents written four and a half years after
the money went in would be exactly the kind of flattery this system exists to
prevent.

## The thing I already knew, on the record, a year ago

From the 1 September 2025 post:

> *"I've been an AR9 shareholder for 5+ years now and every time there is a
> major cyber breach I think to myself... why doesn't (say) Qantas buy AR9's
> Attribute Based Access Control technology? Actually, why doesn't anyone buy
> it but the DoD? The answer being AR9's tech requires some heavy lifting to
> implement."*

That paragraph is the whole bear case, written by me, in my own words, twelve
months ago. It correctly identifies that the addressable *problem* is enormous
and the addressable *market* is small, and it correctly identifies why. The
conclusion drawn from it was to participate in the capital raise.

Knowing the answer and acting on it are different events, and the gap between
them is where the money goes. It is the same pattern as LAU: the credibility
flag raised in month two and acted on in month twenty-eight. Here the market
flag was raised in year five and has not been acted on in year six.

## What would actually settle this

The 2027 US DoD zero-trust mandate. The largest defence buyer on earth
mandating adoption of precisely this category is about as favourable a test as
a thesis can be handed. If AR9 cannot convert *that* into material recurring
multi-year revenue, the product was never the problem and there is nothing
further to wait for.

Everything else — the next $3.2m contract, the next webinar, the next
acquisition — is noise against that one test.
