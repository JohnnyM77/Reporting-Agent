## The number I filed as a risk was the whole thesis

Every version of the entry case leaned on one assumption I never wrote down: that
the realised price per pearl would hold near recent levels. I called it a risk,
noted that FY24 was a record price year, and then built the valuation as if the
record was the base.

Pearls are not a luxury good in any way that matters to a shareholder. They are a
commodity with opaque global pricing, and Atlas is a price and volume taker like
every other primary producer. When the price rolled over 47% in a single half,
there was no moat underneath to catch it, just a farm with a three-to-five-year
production cycle and a lot of fixed cost. That is operating leverage in reverse,
and it is the most predictable thing in the file. The bears in the Strawman
thread said so before it happened: the ultra-low multiple was the market telling
me the last two years of earnings were the top of a cycle, not a new normal.

## The cash was never really mine

The part of the thesis I was proudest of was the balance sheet. Net cash worth a
quarter of the market cap, no debt, downside paid for. What I missed is that cash
on a balance sheet is only protection if the people controlling it are aligned
with me, and this board is not.

About $6m has gone to PT Cahaya Bali, an entity Atlas deconsolidated because it
no longer controls it, including a fresh $1.2m this year while $4.2m was already
provisioned. The money is building a boat Atlas will not own, secured by a
mortgage that does not exist yet, repaid out of charter hire Atlas will pay to
use the boat. We are banker and customer to the same counterparty, in a
jurisdiction the bears flagged as sovereign risk in month one.

And then the remuneration report, which is what actually made me angry. The bonus
formula worked and docked the CEO and CFO to half of maximum. The board then
reached for its "absolute discretion" and paid them back above a full payout, in
a year of statutory loss, a two-thirds dividend cut and a halved share price, and
explained none of it. The rem framework is meant to align management with
shareholders. We lost half our money and they got a raise. That is P4, and it is
the pillar I never bothered to write down because I assumed a debt-free small-cap
run by long-tenured operators would treat the cash as ours. Assumed, not tested.

## Why this is graded C

The 2026 write-ups are genuinely good, and if this file were about 2026 it would
grade higher. But the decision that determined the outcome was the buy in 2024,
and there is no contemporaneous thesis for it, no dated valuation, no written
kill condition, only a handful of scrambled notes in a spreadsheet tab. Grading
it up on the strength of posts written two years after the money went in would be
exactly the flattery this system exists to prevent. It is the same shape as AR9:
excellent later writing about a decision that was never documented when it was made.

## What would actually settle this

The realised price per pearl at the Kobe auctions, quarter by quarter. It is the
input the whole model hangs off, it is the number management cannot spin, and it
is the one thing that tells me whether this is a cycle to wait out or a melting
ice cube to abandon. If it turns up across two consecutive auctions while the
Cahaya exposure stops growing and cash flow goes positive, there is a real
cyclical recovery to own. If it drifts back toward pre-FY23 levels, the bears'
"below 5c" is the honest target and there is nothing left to wait for. Everything
management says about "FY30 output growth" is noise until the price confirms it.
