## Why this file exists at all

Every other thesis in here is an argument that the money will come back with
friends. This one is not, and forcing it into that shape would make it a lie.

The disclaimer I wrote at the time says it plainly: *"This is my excuse for
owning shares in a company with almost zero growth prospects.. but who I have
supported since 1988."* That is the thesis. It was true when I wrote it, it is
true now, and the whole point of a system like this is that it has to be able
to hold a position like this one honestly rather than dressing it up.

So the archetype is **TROPHY_ASSET** — a new one, added for this file. The
existing six all assume the reason to own something is the return. Calling the
Broncos a `COMPOUNDING_MACHINE` would be absurd and calling them a `VALUE_TRAP`
would be both insulting and a judgement I have not actually made.

## What happened

A week before Xander's ninth birthday we were in Brisbane to watch the Broncos
at Lang Park. He found out the club was publicly listed and decided he wanted
to be a part owner.

Each of the boys gets $250 on their birthday that must be invested in shares.
It works like a watchlist that they own a piece of — it gives me a reason to
pump them with news about companies they proudly call themselves part owners
of. Xander chose this one himself, which is the part that makes it work.

## The lesson that actually landed

On 30 April 2025 I had to explain to a nine-year-old how a company's value can
fall 11% — about $11m — in a single day with no announcement, no news and
nothing on the socials.

So we looked at the trades. The Broncos opened the day with a market
capitalisation of exactly $100m and closed at $89m. Total value of every share
traded in the company that day: **$2,230**.

Bear77 filled in the mechanics from his screen: a range from 91c to $1.025
across the session, gaps of clear daylight between the bids and the offers, and
a floor of sorts at 90c that a single $100,000 sell order would go straight
through. That is the entire lesson about what a share price is, delivered by
the market for free, on a company he cares about.

You could not construct a better teaching example on purpose, and it only
worked because he already cared who won on the weekend.

## Why Grade A

The record is contemporaneous, it is mine, and it is dated: the Strawman post
of 30 April 2025, written the day it happened, containing both the reason for
owning the shares and the disclaimer about what that reason is worth.

There is no valuation work in it. On any other file that would cap the grade at
B. Here it is not a gap in the record — the absence of valuation work *is* the
thesis, stated at the time, in writing. Grading it down for failing to do
analysis I explicitly declined to do would be marking the wrong thing.

## The pillar I would defend hardest

**P3 — I do not react to the price, and neither does he.** Its kill condition is
about my behaviour rather than the company's, which makes it the only pillar in
this repository that can be breached by me alone.

That is the correct design for this position. There is no plausible sequence of
Broncos results that damages the reason for owning these shares. There is a
very plausible sequence in which the price does something stupid on $2,000 of
turnover and I do something stupid in response. The kill condition points at
the actual risk.

## The pre-mortem line I want on the record

*"It is my nostalgia wearing his birthday as a disguise."*

I have supported this club since 1988. He was eight when we went to Lang Park.
The sources block credits him with the idea, and I believe that is true — but
it is exactly the kind of thing a parent remembers generously, and in ten
years the honest answer might be different. Writing it down now is the only way
to find out later.

## One correction for the record

The club entered the competition in **1988**, not 1998, and my own post at the
time says I have supported them since 1988. The file uses 1988 throughout.

## What the boys are actually being taught

DroneShield is in this portfolio for the same underlying reason and carries the
same risk in its pre-mortem: a position held as a teaching exercise has a
different sell discipline from one held for return, and the difference does not
announce itself.

The difference here is that BBL says so on the front page instead of hiding it
in the pre-mortem. The lesson worth teaching is not "hold forever" — it is
"write down what would change your mind, then hold until it does." On this file
what would change my mind is Xander losing interest, and that is written down.
