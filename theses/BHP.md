## The Fundão record, and the story I tell about it

BHP is the position I am most likely to misremember, so the sequence is written
down here rather than left to memory.

The Fundão tailings dam at Samarco failed on **5 November 2015**. Two of the
three purchases were made *before* that date. The buy on **14 August 2015** at
$24.49 and the buy on **4 November 2015** at $21.30 were commodity-price
trades — iron ore was collapsing and the market was pricing the cost curve as
though BHP sat in the middle of it. Only the third, on **14 November 2015** at
$18.17, was a reaction to Samarco.

The three decisions returned **15.2%, 17.1% and 19.6%** — an ascending ladder,
because each purchase was made into more fear than the one before it.

The story I tell from memory compresses all of this into a single disaster
narrative: *I bought BHP when the dam broke*. That is a better story. It is
also not what happened, and it flatters the decision in a specific way — it
credits me with one act of contrarian nerve rather than three ordinary
purchases into a falling market, only the last of which required any. The
ledger does not support the story, which is exactly why the ledger renders the
numbers and this file does not.

## Why the bet was not "the iron ore price will recover"

It was never a price forecast. The cost-curve case says that when the price
falls, high-cost supply leaves, and the survivor's volumes and margins are
protected on the way back up. That is a claim about *relative position*, which
BHP discloses, rather than about *price*, which nobody knows. The distinction
is the reason the bear case at 29x FY17 earnings was still an acceptable
outcome: a business that cannot lose money at the bottom of a cycle is being
priced for something other than the cycle.

## The open disagreement with Sodhi

Sodhi's framework had a sell above $45 and he has since published a formal
sell. I have not sold. Two honest readings of that:

1. His framework is a valuation discipline applied to a cyclical, and it is
   correct on its own terms. Cyclicals are sold on price because their earnings
   are not durable.
2. The hold thesis is a different thesis to the entry thesis — potash, copper,
   capital discipline — and it has not been tested against his framework at
   all, because I never wrote it down until now.

Reading 2 is the uncomfortable one. A hold thesis written *after* the position
has worked is exactly the kind of reasoning this system exists to flag, and it
is why the entry slide for BHP deliberately shows nothing about how it turned
out. The pre-mortem above was also written with hindsight, and is marked as
such.
