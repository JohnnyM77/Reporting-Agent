## Why this position exists

This was the **first purchase after joining Strawman** in September 2023, and
the introduction to small and mid-cap investing generally. Before it, the
playbook was blue chips that had run into bad news — BHP after Brazil, ResMed
after Ozempic, Fortescue at $4 — with only one or two start-ups ever attempted.

It was also, and this is the part worth recording properly, **bought for the
boys** — and the genesis was a birthday present, not a stock screen.

It was **Zachary's eleventh birthday**, and the gift was SG$250 to invest in
shares of his own choice, framed around three of Buffett's rules: buy a company
whose product you understand and love, run by management you trust, at a fair
price. We sat down to talk about what he might buy, and he asked — unprompted —
whether he could own a defence company, a military one to be precise. I had
only recently joined Strawman, and I told him there was a company everyone on
there was talking about called DroneShield. Let's have a look. That look is how
we came across it, and we bought it for the first time at **31 cents**.

So Zachary got his parcel at 31 cents for his birthday, and I bought a much
larger one for myself the same day. It became the vehicle to teach both boys
what owning a company is: what a business does, what it might be worth, and what
a holding period feels like when the number on the screen halves twice.

They could not have had a better first lesson, and that is mostly luck — the
idea came from an eleven-year-old wanting a defence stock, not from any edge of
mine.

## The part that is a risk, not a feature

A position held as a teaching exercise has a different sell discipline from a
position held for return, and the difference does not announce itself.

Selling it means ending the lesson. Holding it means the lesson continues. That
is a reason to hold that has nothing to do with DroneShield, and it will keep
being a reason long after the thesis stops working — which is precisely the
shape of thing this system exists to name out loud.

It is in the pre-mortem for that reason: *"it was bought as the boys' first
investment, so it was never sold — the teaching purpose quietly became a
reason not to act, and the lesson they actually learn is that you hold
everything forever."*

The lesson worth teaching is not "hold forever". It is "write down what would
change your mind, then hold until it does." That version is teachable, and it
survives the position being wrong.

## Why Grade B and not A

The three reviews below are excellent and they are mine — the ten-bagger post
of July 2025, the FY25 result in February 2026, and the disclosure post in
March 2026. But the first of them is **twenty months after the money went in**.
There is no contemporaneous November 2023 write-up: no thesis, no valuation, no
sizing note.

Same standard as everywhere else in this system. LAU is Grade A because the
entry post came six weeks after the buy. AR9 is Grade C because the decision
that mattered has no record at all. DRO sits between them.

## What the reviews actually show

A thesis that got *better* over time, which is unusual and worth noting.

- **July 2025** — no kill condition. "Do nothing" is a defensible policy and an
  indefensible substitute for one. `process_score: 7`, `outcome_score: 10` —
  the gap between those two numbers is the whole point of scoring them
  separately.
- **February 2026** — the reverse DCF gets written down, and the honest
  sentence appears: the market isn't pricing nice progress, it's pricing almost
  perfect execution. That is an amendment marked `TIGHTENED`, because a
  growth-rate kill condition was attached to it. This is what a good review
  looks like.
- **March 2026** — P4 is added, not because anything broke, but because the
  disclosure regime changed. Once most wins go unannounced, trusting
  management's disclosure stops being a governance nicety and becomes
  load-bearing.

Two `TIGHTENED` amendments and no `LOOSENED` ones. Compare that with the
pattern the drift engine is built to catch.

## The one thing I would push back on

P4 is marked `STRAINED` rather than intact, and it is the only pillar that is.
Three things point the same way: senior management selling into strength, the
decision to raise the announcement threshold to ~$20m, and a 19.6% move on a
report from an outlet that publishes AI-generated content for retail investors
in ASX defence names.

None of those is damning on its own. Together they mean the market is now
partly pricing information it cannot verify, in a stock that reprices
violently, held at a size where that matters. It is the thing to watch, and it
has nothing to do with drones.
