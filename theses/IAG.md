## Why this file exists at all

Every other thesis in this repo answers "why do you own this?" This one answers
a different question, and it is the more dangerous of the two: **what do you do
about a holding you have already decided you will never sell?**

The options were to write nothing, or to write something honest. Writing
nothing is worse than it looks. A position with no file is a position with no
record of why it is exempt, and "I inherited it" degrades over twenty years
into "it has always been a core holding" — which is a different claim
altogether, and one that will eventually be used to justify buying more.

So the file exists to fix the reason in place. Not to test it. The kill
condition on P1 is the one that matters: it does not fire when IAG does badly,
it fires when I start explaining the holding in a way that isn't true.

## The provenance, stated once and precisely

I bought these shares for my mother at the NRMA demutualisation. When she died
they came to me as an inheritance. Both halves of that are true at once and the
order matters: **they were bought with my money, given to her, and returned to
me by her death.** Not a windfall, and not a purchase either. There is no
category in a portfolio for that, which is roughly the point.

This is graded **C — memory only**. There is no contemporaneous note, no
statement from the time, nothing that would survive a challenge on the detail.
If documents ever surface, the grade goes to B and the specifics get corrected
against them rather than the other way round.

## The archetype is wrong, and that is worth saying

`CYCLICAL_TRADE` is the closest of the six available and it is still a bad fit —
"trade" is precisely what this is not. The archetype list has no entry for
*inherited, never selling*, and rather than force a better-sounding label I
would rather the mismatch sit here in the open.

It does describe the **business** correctly, though. IAG is a general insurer:
pricing cycle, perils, reinsurance, reserve development. Nothing about the
provenance of my shares changes what the company is, and P3 exists so the file
does not pretend otherwise.

## Greensill — what I actually claim, and what I do not

I worked at Clyde & Co alongside the partner running the Greensill matter.

**What that gives me is a prior, not information.** Everything in P2 is drawn
from the public record — IAG's own disclosures, the reported structure of the
April 2019 BCC sale to Tokio Marine, and the reported state of the eleven
case-managed proceedings. Nothing in this file comes from anything I saw or was
told in the course of that work, and nothing should ever be added to it on that
basis. If the only support for a claim here is "I was in the building", the
claim does not belong in the file.

What proximity actually left me with is narrower and worth stating plainly: a
belief that litigation of this shape runs longer, costs more in defence than in
settlement, and resolves less cleanly than the "no net exposure" framing
suggests to a reader who has never watched one of these run. The 2026 Greensill
Bank settlement is consistent with that reading — a confidential settlement on
one of eleven fronts, with roughly $3bn of Credit Suisse and White Oak claims
still live — but it is equally consistent with management being right and the
arrangements holding. **Both readings survive the evidence so far.** That is
why P2 has a resolution date rather than a verdict.

The uncomfortable version, recorded now so it cannot be edited out later: I
would probably hold this belief slightly less strongly if I had not been near
it, and I would hold this stock exactly as hard either way. The belief is doing
no work on the decision. It only does work on how surprised I get to be.

## What Theo should ask about this one

Not "is it still a buy". The useful questions each season are:

1. **Has the reason changed?** If a review defends IAG on valuation or yield,
   P1 is breached — flag it, whatever the number says.
2. **Is it still the size it became by accident?** A never-sell holding is
   never sized. Someone has to look at the weight on purpose.
3. **Is P2 still undecided, or have I started treating it as settled in my
   favour?** An unfalsifiable grudge and an open question look identical from
   the inside.

The one thing this file must never become is a good investment case. If a
future version of it reads like one, something has gone wrong — and it will not
be the company.
