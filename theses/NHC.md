the documents plus the dates show why.

## The second iteration, and why it is flagged

The reason given for holding today is not the reason for buying. The entry bet
was that coal demand outlives the political consensus. The current reason leans
on AI datacentre load outrunning firmed renewable build.

That is a real change in the world and it deserves to be in the file. It is
also precisely the shape of thing this system exists to be suspicious of: a new
supporting argument, discovered after the position is up 34%, for a position
already held. Updating on new information and backfilling justification look
identical from the inside.

The test is whether the new argument arrives with a kill condition or without
one. So the AI leg sits inside P1 under the *existing* kill condition —
thermal below US$90/t for four quarters, or Australian export volumes falling
three years running — rather than getting a softer one of its own. The
amendment is logged as `NEUTRAL` on that basis. If a future review widens that
condition to accommodate the AI story, `direction: LOOSENED` is the correct
label and the system will start counting.

## What changed from the draft

The previous file was Grade C, marked DRAFT, with three pillars whose kill
conditions all read `NEEDS WORK`. Those were honest placeholders and they have
now been replaced with real ones drawn from the documents:

- **P1** was untestable — "coal will last longer than people think" has no

- **P2** and **P3** were assertions; they are now anchored to specific unit
  costs, specific tonnages and specific dates.
- **P4** was the vaguest and is now the sharpest: the thing that would break it
  is a Malabar takeover funded by cutting the dividend, and that risk is named
  in the source documents rather than invented here.

The old file's central worry stands and is worth restating, because writing
better kill conditions does not dissolve it: this position has already worked,
and a kill condition written after the fact is written by someone who wants to
keep holding.

## The risks the documents name that the thesis does not solve

**New Acland's legal position.** Approval took a twelve-year vetting process
and there was still another court hearing scheduled for early 2025. Sodhi's own
words: legal risks are hard to quantify and remain a source of risk. The entire
growth leg of P3 depends on an outcome nobody in this file controls.

**Malabar.** The market speculated New Hope would bid, which would mean using
surplus cash, forgoing dividends and possibly raising equity — the share price
was weak on that fear and jumped 10% when the interim result showed dividends
rising instead. Management have not done it. P4's kill condition exists for the
day they might.
