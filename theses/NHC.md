## Why this file is marked DRAFT and stays that way until it is fixed

"Coal will last longer than people think" is close to untestable on any horizon
short enough to matter. That is precisely what makes it comfortable to believe:
there is no date on which it can be shown to be wrong, so holding it costs
nothing in self-regard. A thesis that cannot be falsified is not a thesis, it
is a mood — and this one has three pillars whose kill conditions currently read
NEEDS WORK, which is the accurate description.

The second problem is worse. **The position has already worked.** Three
decisions have returned 22.0%, 27.8% and 40.3%. That is exactly the moment when
writing an honest kill condition is hardest, because any kill condition strict
enough to be useful now implies selling something that is currently making
money, and any kill condition loose enough to be comfortable is not a kill
condition at all.

So the sequence matters: write the kill conditions *first*, while noting that
the temptation to write them loosely is at its maximum, and only then decide
whether the position size is right.

## What is actually borrowed here

The idea, the framing and most of the confidence. The `origin: BORROWED` field
is not a formality — I have not independently verified the load-growth claim in
P1, the financing claim in P2, or the capital-return record in P3. Until those
three are sourced, "I agree with Gaurav" is the whole of the analysis, and the
`ALIGNED` marker on the source means agreement, not confirmation.

## What would make this a real thesis

1. **P1** — name a grid (NEM, or India's), a measure (thermal share of
   generation, or absolute thermal TWh), and a date by which the claim is
   either holding or not.
2. **P2** — name the observable that would show capital returning: new mine
   approvals above some level, a major lender re-entering, or an equity raise
   for greenfield supply getting away.
3. **P3** — name a payout ratio floor, or a growth-capex ceiling, over a stated
   number of years.

Until then this is a borrowed position that happens to be up, which is the
least informative thing a position can be.
