## What actually happened, in order

The story I would tell from memory is "I followed Gaurav into PWR." The ledger
says something slightly different, and the difference is worth keeping.

| Date | Event | Price |
|---|---|---|
| 24 Feb 2025 | Sodhi upgrades on the interim result | BUY at $7.34 |
| **10 Apr 2025** | **I buy — 4,444 shares, one decision** | **$6.69** |
| 15 Apr 2025 | Sodhi's "a power move" re-rates after the tariff sell-off | BUY at $6.87 |

So the idea was entirely his — the February piece is the reason this position
exists at all. But I did not act on it in February at $7.34. I waited through a
30% drawdown and bought into the tariff panic at $6.69, five days *before* he
published again at a higher price.

That is a better entry than the person I was following, and it is exactly the
kind of detail memory embellishes, so it is worth being precise: it may have
been patience, and it may equally have been that I only got round to it in
April. The file records the sequence; it does not claim to know the motive.

One decision, not three. There was no scaling in, which means there is no
averaging-down story here and no ladder to point at.

## The uncomfortable part: this is a borrowed thesis that has worked

`origin: BORROWED` is not a formality. I did not independently verify the
margin-recovery case in P1, the A&D conversion case in P2, or the pricing-power
case in P3. All three come from two articles. `ALIGNED` means I agreed with
him, not that I confirmed him.

And the position is up — 1.54x, an IRR near 38%. That is precisely when a
borrowed thesis is most dangerous, because being right for someone else's
reasons feels identical to being right.

## The valuation gap, which is now live

Sally has flagged PWH as a Tier 3 trim candidate: 98th valuation percentile,
trailing PE around 67x. Sodhi's published guide, as read in August 2026, still
says HOLD to $20 against a $12.15 price.

So the two sources disagree, and **this thesis contains no valuation kill
condition of its own** — the same gap BHP has. All three pillars are about the
business: margins, division mix, pricing power. None of them says at what price
a good business stops being worth holding.

That question is open and is deliberately not answered here. Answering it is
mine to do, in a dated review, not something the file should quietly assume.
Run `python -m theo.cli signals` to see it stated properly.

## Why COMPOUNDING_MACHINE and not CYCLICAL_TRADE

Arguable. Motorsport is roughly half of revenue and does have a cycle, and the
aftermarket segment is explicitly the cyclical part. But the entry case was not
"buy the cycle low and sell it high" — it was "a quality business is reinvesting
through a trough that has a published end date." The archetype should be
revisited if A&D stalls, because without the structural growth leg this really
is just a cyclical supplier with a good reputation.
