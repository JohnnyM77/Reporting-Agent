## The record

ResMed was on my watchlist before Ozempic was a headline, and for a specific
reason: the 75th-anniversary edition of Benjamin Graham's *The Intelligent
Investor*, updated by Jason Zweig, lists what Zweig calls the thirty greatest
compounders of all time — and ResMed is on it. That is not a name you buy on a
whim; it is a name you file under "great business, wait for a price."

The price came in September 2023. Weight-loss drugs became the story of the
year, someone asked whether Ozempic was the end of ResMed, and the market
marked one of Australia's best healthcare companies down on the fear. Graham
Witcomb upgraded it to BUY at $23.99 on 7 September. I bought a few days later,
at a little under $23 — below even his review price, because the selling was
still running. It has been a great investment.

## Why this position exists

It is the cleanest example in the whole portfolio of my actual playbook, stated
in my own words elsewhere in this system: *invest as much as I could stomach in
blue chips that had run into bad news where the research said there was no real
danger.* BHP after Brazil, Fortescue at $4 — and **ResMed after Ozempic** is
the one I reach for first when I describe how I invest.

The edge, to the extent there was one, was not insight. Witcomb did the
analysis and Zweig had already told me it was a great business. The edge was
temperament: being willing to buy a known compounder into a headline everyone
else was selling, on a watchlist name I had been waiting on for exactly this.

## Why Grade B

The document that underpins this thesis existed at the time of the decision —
the 7 September 2023 upgrade, five days before I bought — which is what
separates B from C. But there is no contemporaneous write-up of *my own*: no
note, no valuation work, no record of the decision beyond the transaction
itself.

Same standard as everywhere in this system. LAU is Grade A because the entry
post came six weeks after the buy; AR9 is Grade C because the decision that
mattered has no record at all. RMD sits with ABB, DRO, NHC, PWH and MSV in the
middle — reconstructed now, but from a document that was real then.

## The pillar I would defend hardest

**P3 — bought cheap on a transient fear, with the real risk correctly named.**

The article's most valuable sentence is not about Ozempic at all. It is that
the bigger question mark, in its own words, is Philips returning to the market
and restarting the price war it paused when its product recall sidelined it.
Ozempic was the headline; Philips was the risk. The upgrade cut its own maximum
weighting from 7% to 5% for exactly that reason.

So the kill condition on P3 is about margins, not miracle drugs. If Philips'
return knocks a couple of points off margin, that is the price of the trade and
was disclosed. If it turns into two consecutive years of profit growth lagging
revenue growth — the pre-pandemic pattern the industry always had — then the
thing I bought has changed, and no amount of Ozempic being a non-event saves
the thesis.

## The pre-mortem line I want on the record

*"Betteridge's Law is a wonderful line and a terrible risk model."*

Buying because a bear case is overblown is the right call far more often than
not, and it has been the right call here. But it is a temperament, not a
process, and the one time the headline question's answer is genuinely "yes" is
the time it costs the most. Writing that down is the difference between a
playbook and a lucky streak.
