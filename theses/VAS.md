## What this is, and what it is not

VAS is not a conviction bet on a business; it is the opposite of one. It is
broad Australian market exposure — the few hundred largest ASX companies — held
cheaply, bought because I had capital I wanted invested and no specific
single-stock idea I wanted to back with it.

It has been an **okay investment, not an out-of-this-world one**, which is
exactly what it is supposed to be. An index fund's job is to reliably be the
market, and it has done that.

## Why Grade C

There is no research document behind this and there was never meant to be —
buying the index is a decision to *not* analyse. It is reconstructed from memory
and the ledger, which is the honest definition of Grade C. That is not a
weakness here the way it would be for a single stock; it simply reflects that
the "thesis" is diversification itself.

## The one honest caveat

VAS and VHY are both Australian equities, so holding both is less diversifying
than two separate tickers imply — VHY is really a high-yield slice of the same
market. The genuine diversification in this group comes from VEU, which is
deliberately the rest of the world. Worth remembering when I think of these
three as "spread out".
