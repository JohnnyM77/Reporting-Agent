## What this is

VHY is the income tilt — a basket of higher-yielding Australian companies held
cheaply for franked dividends and diversification. Like VAS and VEU it was
parked capital: money put to work without a specific single-stock idea behind
it, chosen here for its income character.

It has been an **okay, income-paying holding — not spectacular**, which is what
a yield-tilted index fund is for.

## The two honest caveats

**It doubles up with VAS.** Both are Australian equities; VHY is a high-yield
slice of the same market. Holding both is less diversifying than the two tickers
imply — the real geographic diversification in this group is VEU.

**Yield screens can be value traps.** A fund that selects for high dividend
yield will, by construction, tend to pick up companies whose yield is high
because the market has given up on their growth. That is a feature to watch, not
a reason to avoid it, but it is why this is a diversifier and not a conviction
holding.

## Why Grade C

No research document and none intended — an index tilt is a decision not to
analyse individual names. Reconstructed from memory and the ledger, which is
Grade C by definition and exactly right for what this is.
